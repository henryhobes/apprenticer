Apprenticer

Talk to an AI about anything, by voice, in your browser, with a board beside it that shows what is easier to see than to hear.

You don't configure it. Your coding agent writes a space, a plain folder; Apprenticer serves it on your computer and you talk. The space's brief decides how the AI, which Apprenticer calls the mind, behaves: nothing about teaching or interviewing is built in. This page shows one space, a 20-minute lesson on why AI runs on GPUs. It is a static page: there is no AI on it, and nothing it shows was said in a session.

  • Voice and a boardYou talk; you hear the answer; the board beside it puts up the visual the answer is about, and moves it as the words are said.
  • The mind writes every wordThe voice model only listens and talks. Every reply, and every board move, is written by a text model that has read the whole space.
  • A space is a folderA brief in plain markdown, the visuals as HTML files, the sources it may draw on. Your coding agent writes it with you.
  • Notes give it memoryWhen you end a session, the mind rewrites its notes, so next time it knows where you left off.

The lesson: What is a GPU, and why does AI run on one?

The brief sets the goal:

Twenty minutes, by voice, with Alex. Alex walks away able to say, in their own words: a GPU is not a faster CPU, it wins only when the work splits into many independent pieces doing the same step; matrix multiply, the core of AI, is exactly that kind of work; the usual limit is memory, not arithmetic; and that is why a chatbot uses a sliver of an H100, and what batching and quantization do about it.

Alex is the learner the brief is written for: someone who works in finance, reads about AI chips every week, and has never written code. The arc is four predictions, each asked before the board shows the answer, then an explain-back:

  1. 0 to 6 minLanes, 0 to 6 minutes: why thousands of lanes beat 96 cores only on one kind of work
  2. 6 to 10 minMatrix multiply, 6 to 10 minutes: the shape of work AI is made of
  3. 10 to 14 minMemory, 10 to 14 minutes: the chip is usually waiting on memory, not arithmetic
  4. 14 to 18 minThe chatbot, 14 to 18 minutes: why one chatbot uses a sliver of an H100
  5. 18 to 20 minThe close: At about 18 minutes, wherever you are, go to the close.

Gold-marked quotes are from brief.md, word for word: the brief's own words. No session has been recorded, so nothing here is a transcript.

The board

These are the space's four visuals, built from its visuals/ folder. In a session each part comes up as the tutor names it, and the tutor makes a move with a board line such as BOARD: set mixed. Here every part is already up: press the moves to step through them, as the tutor would.

1 Lanes, 0 to 6 minutes

It opens on this board and a prediction:

Two chips. On the left, a server processor: ninety-six cores, each thinking for itself. On the right, an AI chip: almost seventeen thousand lanes that must all take the same step together. Give both the same pile of work, split into a hundred different kinds of job. Which finishes first?

Then the moves answer it. set mixed: a hundred kinds of job, and the lanes can only do one kind at a time, so it's about even. set same: one kind of work, and the GPU takes one tick.

2 Matrix multiply, 6 to 10 minutes

Each answer cell is one row times one column, and no cell waits on any other. The prediction:

If every cell got its own lane, how long would all sixty-four take, compared with one cell?

set all lights every cell at once and brings up the real size. AI is almost all this one shape of work.

3 Memory, 10 to 14 minutes

Lanes can only work on numbers that have arrived from memory: the roofline shows both limits and the ridge where they meet. After set matmul:

At that dot, what is the chip waiting on: memory or arithmetic?

The same board comes back in the next beat for set chatbot and set batch, and in the close with set labels off: their buttons are here.

4 The chatbot, 14 to 18 minutes

Every token a chatbot writes reads all of its weights from memory. The prediction:

If each weight took one byte instead of two, what happens to the speed?

set int8: twice as fast with no extra arithmetic. Then back to the roofline: set chatbot puts the dot at a fraction of a percent of peak, and set batch slides it up to the ridge, because one read of the weights serves every user in the batch.

5 The close

The roofline goes up with its labels hidden (set labels off), and the brief asks for the explain-back:

In your own words, why does a chatbot use so little of this chip?

As Alex names the parts, the labels come back. Then one more, over the lanes:

So why isn't a GPU just a faster CPU?

Next time, the notes say which ideas Alex explained back, and the tutor will Open on the first open one with its visual and its prediction question, no recap.

The space is a folder

Everything above comes from these files. Change the brief and the tutor changes; there is no other configuration.

  • brief.mdwho the tutor is, the goal, the arc, the rules. Given to the mind word for word
  • notes/memory.mdits memory of Alex, rewritten after every session
  • persona.mda simulated Alex, for rehearsing the space in text with apprenticer sim
  • sources/gpu-facts.mdthe facts every number comes from
  • visuals/01-lanes.htmlthe four boards below
  • visuals/02-matmul.html
  • visuals/03-roofline.html
  • visuals/04-chatbot.html

Try it yourself

It runs on your computer, in your browser, and talks to OpenAI with your own API key. It needs Node 24 or later.

git clone https://github.com/henryhobes/apprenticer && cd apprenticer
npm install -g apprenticer
apprenticer ./examples/what-is-a-gpu

The first run asks for your OPENAI_API_KEY once and saves it to ~/.config/apprenticer/.env. The page opens; tap On, allow the microphone, and the tutor speaks first. Tap Off when you're done.

A session costs about $2 to $3 on your key. The page shows what the session has cost, and a new session is refused once a space has spent $20 in a day.

To see the boards without a key: apprenticer preview ./examples/what-is-a-gpu. To make your own space: apprenticer skill, then tell your coding agent what you want to learn or rehearse.
